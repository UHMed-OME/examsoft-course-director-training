Section 02

Set your preferences first#

The first thing to do in the portal. Two fields, one Save button.

JABSOM convention

Set both font fields to Arial 12

ExamSoft ships with its own defaults. JABSOM standardizes on Arial 12 so that questions written by different course directors over nine years still look the same when they land in one assessment. Do this before you write or import anything.

Steps

  1. Open the account menu Hover the gear icon at the top right of any portal page, then choose My Preferences.
  2. Go to the Question Creation tab First of the four tabs across the top.
  3. Set Question Fonts Under General Options (All Question Types), choose Arial, size 12.
  4. Set Answer Fonts Under Multiple Choice Answers, choose Arial, size 12.
  5. Save Click Save Preferences. Revert to Default discards the change, so avoid that button.

Leave the other tabs alone. Partial Credit and Number of Answer Choices are decisions you make per question while writing, so there is no reason to change them globally now.

Section 03

The JABSOM exam cycle#

The schedule your work sits inside. Read this before you agree to an exam date.

None of this comes from ExamSoft. It is how OME runs exams, and it exists because an exam delivered on seventy-odd iPads has to be proofed, previewed on a real device, printed as a backup, and wrapped in proctor instructions before a student ever opens it.

  1. 7 business days before Draft complete You

    Questions written, ordered, categorized, and attached to the assessment. This is the handoff.

  2. 7 to 2 business days before Proofing OME staff

    Staff preview the assessment on an iPad, come back to you on anything unclear, fix errors, and preview again. They print two hard copies and build the proctor PowerPoint.

  3. 36 to 48 hours before Posted OME staff

    Posting opens the download window. Students need the time to pull the exam onto their iPads.

  4. Exam day Password announced Proctor

    Students cannot open the exam until then. Examplify locks the iPad for the duration.

  5. Minutes after upload Results visible You

    Once answer files upload, the assessment overview shows you how the class did.

Critical

It is more than just clicking a button

That line is from the 2020 OME onboarding document and it has survived every revision since. The proofing steps run in sequence and they need the full 7 days. A late draft compresses proofing. It does not move the exam date.

Watch out

Fix content problems before the download window opens

Once the assessment is posted and one student has downloaded it, you can no longer change questions or answer keys. After that point an error becomes a scoring adjustment.

Section 04

Writing questions#

Where questions live, how to make one, and the tagging rule that is not optional here.

Questions live in the question bank. Get there from the Questions menu at the top of any page, or from Create Questions on the portal home page. A question is an object in the bank. An assessment is a selection of those objects.

The tagging rule

JABSOM convention

Tag every question with at least one category

The software will not force you to do this. JABSOM does. An untagged question is invisible to category reporting, which is how the school looks at performance by topic across a course and across years.

You can tag while writing a question using the Add or Remove Category button, then search or scroll the tree and click the green plus sign. To fix a batch of untagged questions, tick their checkboxes in the questions table, choose Questions Bulk Edit Options at the bottom of the screen, then Change Category Assignments.

Watch out

Apply tags, but leave the tree alone

The category tree is shared across every course and about nine years of historical items. Renaming or moving a node quietly changes what past reports mean. Your account is set to restricted on Categories for that reason. If your course needs a category that does not exist, ask OME to add it.

Worth knowing while you write

  • Set your fonts first. See Set your preferences first.
  • Partial Credit on a multiple-answer question also caps how many choices a student can pick. Two correct answers means they can select two.
  • +/- Partial Credit adds points for correct picks and subtracts for wrong ones. Say so in the stem if you use it, so students do not guess blind.
  • Fill in the Blank allows up to 15 blanks per question. Separate acceptable alternative spellings with a vertical pipe.

Section 05

Question types#

What the Legacy portal offers, what OME prefers, and the two types worth learning properly.

Question types and where OME stands on each
TypeOME stanceNotes
Multiple ChoicePreferredDoes most of the work. Handles one or several correct answers, with partial credit options.
Multiple SelectPreferredIn the Legacy portal this is Multiple Choice with more than one answer marked correct, not a separate type.
True / FalseFineTwo options. Discriminates poorly, so use it sparingly.
Fill in the BlankNot preferableSpelling is scored. Up to 15 blanks per question, with alternatives separated by a vertical pipe.
MatchingNot preferablePairs prompts with responses.
Hot SpotGood for image questionsRectangle and polygon variants. The student drops a pin on the image.
Drag and DropGood, with careAllows partial credit with negative scoring. Does not score sequence.
JABSOM convention

Not preferable is an OME judgment, not a limitation

Fill in the Blank and Matching both work. OME discourages them because they produce more scoring disputes and more post-exam adjustment than they are worth. If your course has a good reason to use one, raise it before the draft deadline rather than at proofing.

Hot Spot

The student taps a region of an image and a pin drops there. This is the best item type JABSOM has for anatomy, neuroanatomy, and clinical skills, or anywhere the answer is a location rather than a word.

  • Pick rectangle or polygon first. Rectangle is quicker to define. Polygon is worth the effort on irregular structures.
  • Add the image by dragging it onto the question or selecting the box below the question text. GIF, JPG, JPEG, and PNG all work.
  • Drag to outline the correct region. To move a hot spot later, press and hold, then drag it.
  • Students can pinch to zoom on the iPad, so small structures stay answerable.

Drag and Drop

The student reads a list of possible answers and drags the correct ones into an answer box. Two things about it catch people out.

Critical

Drag and Drop does not score sequence

The order a student drags answers into the box makes no difference to the score. If you need sequence scored, use the Ordering type instead.

Watch out

Partial credit here subtracts points

With partial credit on, a student gains points for each correct answer dragged in and loses the same amount for each wrong one. The score stops at 0 rather than going negative. Warn them in the stem, for example: answer carefully, because dragging incorrect answers into the box can reduce credit. Without that warning students guess and the item stops behaving predictably.

Keep answer choices short. You can put images inside a choice, but large or multiple images render badly.

To do, maintainer

The Legacy portal also offers Ordering and Matrix question types. Neither is in the OME list above and neither appears in any onboarding document, but Ordering is in the student-facing orientation deck, so students may meet it. Decide whether OME wants to sanction either type for pre-clerkship exams and whether to document them here.

Section 06

Attachments and media#

What you can attach, where it goes, and the limits that will stop you.

Attachments work at two levels. An assessment-level attachment stays available for the whole exam, which suits a formulary, a lab reference sheet, or a case study. You set it on the Assessment Options page, students reach it any time through the Exam Controls menu in Examplify, and you get exactly one per assessment. A question-level attachment only opens while the student is on that question, and a question can carry several.

Watch out

Only one file at assessment level

If your exam needs several shared references, combine them into one file first, usually a single PDF. Work that out before the draft deadline rather than during OME proofing.

Critical

200 MB total per assessment

That ceiling covers every attachment and every inline image in the assessment added together, not each file separately. An image-heavy exam will reach it. If you are building one, budget for it early.

Per-file limits by type
TypeFormatsMaximum sizeOther limits
ImagesGIF, JPG, JPEG, PNG3 MB1920 x 1080 pixels maximum
AudioMP3, MP4 (audio)15 MBNone
VideoMP4, AVI, MOV150 MBOne 150 MB video eats most of the 200 MB budget
PDFPDF20 MBVendor-documented, not in the OME onboarding docs
RTFRTF5 MBVendor-documented, not in the OME onboarding docs
Note

Where these numbers come from

The image, audio, and video limits appear in both the OME onboarding documents and the vendor article. The PDF and RTF limits come from the vendor article alone, since the OME documents never listed them. They should be accurate, but JABSOM has not exercised them.

Worth knowing

  • Resize images to 1920 x 1080 or smaller before uploading. An oversized file gets rejected rather than resized for you.
  • Radiology and histology images are usually what pushes an exam toward the 200 MB ceiling. Compress rather than crop when diagnostic detail matters.
  • Video plays on the device in a secure exam. Test it on an iPad during OME preview, because desktop playback tells you very little.

Section 07

Building an assessment#

How JABSOM actually does it: copy last year, then edit. Starting fresh is rare.

JABSOM convention

Start from last year's exam

With about nine years of items on the system, most JABSOM assessments get built by copying the previous year's exam and editing it. That keeps question order, categorization, and scoring setup intact, along with the institutional memory of what worked.

Copy and repost are different things

Two ways to reuse an assessment
Duplicate (separate assessment)Repost (linked to the original)
New Exam IDYesNo
Can change questionsYesNo
Can change scoring and optionsYesNo
Can set new password and datesYesYes
ReportingIts own reportsCombines data from all postings
Use it forNext year's versionA make-up sitting or a missed cohort
Watch out

For a new academic year, duplicate

A repost folds this year's results into last year's reports, which ruins year-over-year comparison. Duplicating gives the new exam its own Exam ID and its own reporting.

Working order

  1. Duplicate last year's assessment Name it with the academic year so the next person can find it.
  2. Remove retired questions Anything the course no longer teaches, plus items that performed badly in last year's analysis.
  3. Add and tag new questions Write them in the bank first, then add them. Every new question needs at least one category.
  4. Check order and scoring Confirm point values, and that partial credit settings survived the copy.
  5. Attach what the exam needs Mind the 200 MB ceiling. See Attachments and media.
  6. Hand off to OME 7 business days before the exam. Do not post it yourself unless OME asked you to.

OME normally does this. It is here so you know what gets set for you, and what you cannot change afterwards.

Note

Posting happens during the preparation window. Do not post your own exam unless OME has asked you to. If you do, every field below matters.

Posting is what opens the download window. Open the assessment, scroll to the end, and select Post Assessment. A dialog opens with these fields.

Critical

Max Downloads is always 1

Of everything on this dialog, this is the one hard JABSOM convention rather than a judgment call. It has been 1 in every onboarding document since 2020.

One download freezes the content

Once the assessment is posted and at least one student has downloaded it, questions and answer keys lock. An error found after that becomes a post-exam scoring adjustment instead of an edit. Catch content problems during OME proofing.

The password never goes in an email

Posting usually triggers a general notification so students know the exam is available to download. The password stays out of it. The proctor announces it in the room.

Section 09

Exam day#

What the app does to the iPad, and what happens when a student gets locked out.

Students sit the exam in Examplify, the ExamSoft app for iPad. You will not usually be in the room, but the questions students ask you afterwards will be about what happens in it.

The iPad locks down

Once the exam starts, Examplify takes over the device. Screenshots, copy and paste, and internet access all stop working until the exam ends. Nothing else on the iPad is reachable.

Watch out

Button combinations can lock a student out

Press enough button combinations on the iPad and Examplify locks the student out of the exam. Getting back in needs a universal code, also called an exam resume code. The proctor holds it, printed on the PowerPoint OME prepares for that sitting.

Note

Codes are not in this repository

Exam passwords and universal resume codes belong to a specific sitting and live on the OME proctor materials for it. They are never committed here.

What students are told beforehand

  • Charge the iPad fully.
  • Shut it down and restart it at least once, which clears most technical problems before they happen.
  • Download the exam when the window opens rather than on the morning of the exam.

The fallback

OME prints two hard copies of every exam, which cover device failure and in-house exam takers. A student whose iPad fails mid-exam should tell the proctor straight away rather than troubleshoot, because the paper copy and the resume code are both in the room.

Note

Nobody can open the exam early

A student can hold the downloaded file for 48 hours and still not open it. The exam stays sealed until the proctor announces the password.

Section 10

After the exam#

Results arrive faster than most people expect. What to look at, and in what order.

Note

You can see performance within minutes

As soon as students upload their answer files, the assessment overview shows you how the class did. There is no batch process to wait for and nothing OME has to send you.

What to look at, in order

  1. Assessment overview Distribution and overall performance. Your check that the exam behaved the way you intended.
  2. Item analysis Find questions the whole class missed, and questions where the distractors did not separate strong students from weak ones. These are your candidates for a scoring adjustment now and for retirement next year.
  3. Category performance This is what the tags were for. A report can show category performance as a summary or by exam taker, which tells you whether a topic landed across the cohort.
Watch out

Talk to OME before adjusting scores

If an item was defective you can accept a second correct answer or drop the item. Do it in consultation with OME rather than on your own, because it changes the record for every student in the cohort.

There is a loop here worth noticing. The item analysis you run this year is what tells you which questions to cut when you duplicate the exam next year. See Building an assessment.

Your account and permissions#

What OME sets on your account and why. Useful when something you expected to see is missing.

OME configures your access under Main Access Rights when the account is created. Most areas offer full or restricted, and restricted can be scoped to particular folders rather than the whole institution. Below is the standard setup for a new pre-clerkship course director.

Standard Main Access Rights for a new course director
AreaSettingReason
AdministrationRestrictedPortal access without the ability to create other users.
QuestionsFull, or scoped to your foldersYou need to write and edit items.
AssessmentsFull, or scoped to your foldersYou need to build and edit assessments for your course.
CategoriesRestrictedOn purpose. The tree is shared across all historical data, so you apply tags rather than restructure it.
RubricsRestrictedMost pre-clerkship courses do not use them.
GradingUncheckedSee the warning below.
ReportsGrantedYou need reporting for performance review and item analysis.
External IDYour UH SSO identifierThis is what lets single sign-on find your account.
Critical

Grading stays unchecked

Ticking Grading turns the account into a grader-only account, which takes away access a course director needs. The symptom is being able to see grading queues but not the question bank or the assessment builder. If that happens, email OME.

Watch out

Why Categories is restricted

About nine years of items are tagged against the shared category tree. Renaming or moving a node quietly changes what past reports mean. If your course needs a category that does not exist, ask OME to add it.

When the External ID does not match what UH single sign-on sends, sign-in fails with the account-not-found error in Account and sign-in. That is the most common access problem by a distance.
