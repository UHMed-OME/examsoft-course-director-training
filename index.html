Section 01

Start here#

What this page is, what you are responsible for, and the three things to do in your first week.

You are a co-course director. ExamSoft is the system JABSOM uses to write exam questions, assemble them into an assessment, deliver it on student iPads, and report on performance. You will spend most of your time in two places: the question bank and the assessment built from it.

You do not run the exam alone. OME pre-clerkship staff handle preview, proofing, printing, proctor materials, and posting. Your job is the academic content and the deadline.

Critical

The one deadline that matters

Your assessment draft is due 7 business days before the exam date. Everything downstream is scheduled off that date. See The JABSOM exam cycle.

Your first week

  1. Confirm you can sign in Your account is provisioned to your @hawaii.edu address and uses UH single sign-on. There is no separate ExamSoft password. See Account and sign-in.
  2. Set your font preferences Do this before you write a single question. It is a two-field change that prevents inconsistent formatting across the whole bank. See Set your preferences first.
  3. Open last year's exam for your course Most JABSOM assessments are not written from scratch. Read last year's before you plan this year's. See Building an assessment.

What this page does not cover

  • Student-facing instructions. Those live in the OME student orientation deck for Examplify on iPad.
  • Grading workflows for essay or rubric-scored items. Ask OME if your course needs them.
  • Anything specific to the Enterprise portal. JABSOM is on Legacy and the screens differ.

Section 03

Set your preferences first#

The first thing a new JABSOM course director does in the portal. Two fields, one Save.

JABSOM convention

JABSOM convention, not an ExamSoft default

ExamSoft ships with its own font defaults. JABSOM standardizes on Arial 12 so that questions written by different course directors across nine years of item data render consistently when they are pulled into the same assessment. Do this before you write or import anything.

Steps

  1. Open the account menu Hover the gear icon at the top right of any portal page, then choose My Preferences.
  2. Go to the Question Creation tab It is the first of the tabs across the top: Question Creation, Assessment Creation, Graders, Reporting.
  3. Set General Options (All Question Types) Set Question Fonts to Arial, size 12.
  4. Set Multiple Choice Answers Set Answer Fonts to Arial, size 12.
  5. Save Click Save Preferences. Not Revert to Default — that discards the change.

The other preference tabs can stay at their defaults. Partial Credit and Number of Answer Choices are per-question decisions you make while writing, not global settings you need to change now.

Section 04

The JABSOM exam cycle#

The schedule your work sits inside. Read this before you plan an exam date.

This is the part that is genuinely JABSOM-specific. ExamSoft imposes none of it. It exists because a delivered exam on 70-odd iPads has to be proofed, previewed on the real device, printed as a fallback, and wrapped in proctor instructions before a student ever sees it.

  1. 7 business days before Assessment draft complete Course director

    All questions written, ordered, categorized, and attached to the assessment. This is the handoff point. Hit it as close as you can.

  2. 7 to 2 business days before Preview, proof, correct, re-preview OME pre-clerkship staff

    Staff preview the assessment on an iPad, review it, come back to you on anything unclear, fix errors, and preview again. They print two hard copies for emergencies and in-house exam takers, and prepare the proctor PowerPoint that carries the exam information.

  3. 36 to 48 hours before Assessment posted OME pre-clerkship staff

    Posting opens the download window so students have time to pull the exam onto their iPads. Download start is usually 48 hours prior.

  4. Exam day Password announced, exam taken Proctor

    Students cannot open the exam until the proctor announces the password. Examplify locks the device down for the duration.

  5. Minutes after upload Performance visible Course director

    Once answer files upload, the assessment overview in the portal gives you a first read on how the class did.

Critical

"It is more than just clicking a button"

That line is from the original OME onboarding document and it has survived every revision since 2020. Preview, review, correction, re-preview, printing, and proctor prep are sequential and they need the full 7 business days. A draft that lands late compresses the proofing window, not the exam date.

Watch out

The hard lock after posting

Once an assessment is posted and at least one student has downloaded it, you can no longer alter questions or answer keys. Content corrections have to happen before the download window opens.

Section 05

Writing questions#

Where questions live, how to create one, and the tagging rule that is not optional at JABSOM.

Questions live in the question bank, reached from the Questions menu at the top of any portal page, or from Create Questions on the portal home page. A question is an object in the bank; an assessment is a selection of those objects. Writing a question does not put it on an exam.

The tagging rule

JABSOM convention

At least one category on every question

This is a JABSOM requirement, enforced by practice rather than by the software. Every question must carry at least one category tag. Without it the question is invisible to category reporting, which is how the school evaluates performance by topic across a course and across years.

You can tag while writing a question using the Add or Remove Category button, then search or scroll the tree and select the green plus sign. If you are fixing a batch of untagged questions, select their checkboxes in the questions table, choose Questions Bulk Edit Options at the bottom of the screen, then Change Category Assignments.

Watch out

Apply tags. Do not restructure the tree.

The category tree is shared across every course and roughly nine years of historical item data. Renaming or moving a node changes the meaning of past reports. Your account is deliberately set to restricted on Categories for this reason. If the tree is missing something your course needs, ask OME rather than adding it yourself.

Practical notes

  • Set your fonts before you start. See Set your preferences first.
  • Partial Credit on a multiple-answer question also restricts how many choices a student can select — two selectable when there are two correct answers, and so on.
  • +/- Partial Credit awards credit for correct selections and subtracts for incorrect ones. If you use it, say so in the question stem so students do not guess blind.
  • Fill in the Blank supports up to 15 blanks per question, and alternative accepted spellings are separated by a vertical pipe character.

Section 06

Question types#

What the Legacy portal offers, what OME prefers, and the two types worth learning properly.

Question types and the OME position on each
TypeOME stanceNotes
Multiple ChoicePreferredThe workhorse. Supports one or many correct answers, with partial credit options.
Multiple SelectPreferredIn the Legacy portal this is Multiple Choice with more than one correct answer marked, not a separate item type.
True / FalseFineTwo options. Low discrimination, so use sparingly.
Fill in the BlankNot preferableSpelling is scored. Up to 15 blanks per question; alternative spellings separated by a vertical pipe.
MatchingNot preferablePairs prompts with responses.
Hot SpotStrong for image-based itemsRectangle and polygon variants. Student drops a pin on the image.
Drag and DropStrong, with careSupports partial credit with negative scoring. Not an ordering question.
JABSOM convention

Not preferable is an OME judgement, not a limitation

Fill in the Blank and Matching both work in the portal. OME discourages them because they generate more scoring disputes and more post-exam adjustment work than they are worth. If your course has a real reason to use one, raise it before the draft deadline rather than at proofing.

Hot Spot

The student answers by tapping a region of an image, which drops a pin. This is the strongest item type JABSOM has for anatomy, neuroanatomy, and clinical skills — anything where the answer is a location rather than a word.

  • Two variants: rectangle and polygon. Rectangle is faster to define; polygon is worth the effort for irregular structures.
  • Add the image by dragging it onto the question or selecting the box below the question text. GIF, JPG, JPEG, and PNG are accepted.
  • Choose the variant first, then drag to outline the region. A hot spot can be repositioned by pressing and holding, then dragging it.
  • Students can pinch to zoom on the iPad, so fine structures stay answerable.

Drag and Drop

The student reviews a list of possible answers and drags the correct ones into an answer box. Two properties matter, and both are routinely misunderstood.

Critical

Drag and Drop is not an ordering question

The sequence in which a student drags answers into the box does not matter. If sequence needs to be scored, this is the wrong item type — the Legacy portal has a separate Ordering type for that.

Watch out

Partial credit here can subtract

With partial credit enabled, a student earns points for each correct answer dragged in and loses the same amount for each incorrect one. The question score is floored at 0, so it never goes negative. Because of that penalty, say so in the stem — for example: Answer carefully, dragging incorrect answers into the box can reduce credit for this question. Otherwise students guess and the item behaves unpredictably.

Keep answer choices short. Multiple or large images inside a drag-and-drop choice are supported but render badly.

To do — maintainer

The Legacy portal also offers Ordering and Matrix question types. Neither is in the OME list above and neither appears in any onboarding document, but Ordering does appear in the student-facing orientation deck, so students may encounter it. Decide whether OME wants to sanction either type for pre-clerkship exams and whether to document them here.

Section 07

Attachments and media#

What you can attach, where, and the ceilings that will stop you.

Attachments work at two levels. An assessment-level attachment is available to the student for the whole exam — a formulary, a lab reference sheet, a case study. It is set on the Assessment Options page, the student reaches it at any time through the Exam Controls menu in Examplify, and you get exactly one of them per assessment. A question-level attachment is only reachable while the student is on that question, and a question can carry more than one.

Watch out

One file at assessment level

If your exam needs several shared references, they have to be combined into a single file — typically one PDF — because the assessment accepts only one. Plan that before the draft deadline rather than discovering it during OME proofing.

Critical

200 MB total per assessment

The ceiling covers all attachments and inline images in the assessment combined, not each file. A question bank heavy with high-resolution anatomy images will hit this. If you are building an image-dense exam, budget for it early rather than discovering it at the draft deadline.

Per-file limits by type
TypeFormatsMaximum sizeOther limits
ImagesGIF, JPG, JPEG, PNG3 MB1920 x 1080 pixels maximum resolution
AudioMP3, MP4 (audio)15 MB—
VideoMP4, AVI, MOV150 MBOne 150 MB video consumes most of the 200 MB assessment budget
PDFPDF20 MBVendor-documented; not in the OME onboarding docs
RTFRTF5 MBVendor-documented; not in the OME onboarding docs
Note

Where these numbers come from

Images, audio, and video limits are confirmed by both the OME onboarding documents and the vendor article. The PDF and RTF limits come from the vendor article only — the OME documents never listed them. Treat them as accurate but unexercised at JABSOM.

Practical notes

  • Downscale images to the 1920 x 1080 ceiling before uploading. An oversized file is rejected rather than resized.
  • Radiology and histology images are the usual reason an exam approaches the 200 MB ceiling. Compress rather than crop if diagnostic detail matters.
  • Video in a secure exam is delivered on the device. Test it on an iPad during OME preview — do not assume desktop playback is representative.

Section 08

Building an assessment#

How JABSOM actually does it: copy last year, then edit. Writing from scratch is the exception.

JABSOM convention

Start from last year's exam

With roughly nine years of item data on the system, most JABSOM assessments are created by copying the previous year's exam and editing it, not by building a new one. This is the default path. It preserves question order, categorization, scoring setup, and the institutional memory of what worked.

Copy or repost: they are different things

Two ways to reuse an assessment
Duplicate (separate assessment)Repost (linked to the original)
New Exam IDYesNo
Can change questionsYesNo
Can change scoring and optionsYesNo
Can set new password and datesYesYes
ReportingIts own reports, separate from the originalReports combine data from all postings
Use it forNext year's version of the examRe-running the same exam for a make-up or a missed cohort
Watch out

For a new academic year, duplicate — do not repost

A repost folds this year's results into last year's reports, which corrupts year-over-year comparison. Duplicating gives the new exam its own Exam ID and its own reporting.

Working order

  1. Duplicate last year's assessment Give it a name that carries the academic year so it is findable next time.
  2. Remove retired questions Anything the course no longer teaches, or items that performed badly in last year's item analysis.
  3. Add and tag new questions Write them in the bank first, then add them to the assessment. Every new question needs at least one category.
  4. Check order and scoring Confirm point values and that partial-credit settings survived the copy.
  5. Attach anything the exam needs Watch the 200 MB ceiling. See Attachments and media.
  6. Hand off to OME 7 business days before the exam date. Do not post it yourself unless OME has asked you to.

Critical

Of every field on this dialog, this is the one that is a hard JABSOM convention rather than a judgement call. It has been 1 in every onboarding document since 2020.

Once the assessment is posted and at least one student has downloaded it, questions and answer keys can no longer be altered. If you find an error after that point it becomes a post-exam scoring adjustment, not an edit. Catch content problems during the OME proofing window.

Watch out

When an assessment is posted, a general notification email usually goes out so students know it is available to download. The exam password is not in it and must not be. The proctor announces the password in the room at exam time.

Section 10

Exam day#

What the software does to the device, and what happens when a student gets locked out.

Students take the exam in Examplify, the ExamSoft app on the iPad. You will not normally be in the room, but you should know how it behaves, because the questions you get afterwards will be about this.

The device is locked down

Once the exam starts, Examplify takes over the iPad. Screenshots, copy and paste, and internet access are all blocked for the duration. Nothing else on the device is reachable.

Watch out

Button combinations can lock a student out

If a student presses too many button combinations on the iPad, Examplify locks them out of the exam. Recovery requires a universal code, also called an exam resume code. The proctor holds it. It is printed on the proctor PowerPoint that OME prepares for every exam.

Note

Codes are not in this repository

Exam passwords and universal resume codes are per-exam and are never committed here. They live on the OME-prepared proctor materials for that specific sitting.

What students are told to do beforehand

  • Charge the iPad fully before the exam.
  • Fully shut down and restart the iPad at least once, which clears most technical problems before they happen.
  • Download the exam as soon as the window opens rather than the morning of.

The fallback

OME prints two hard copies of every exam. They cover device failure and in-house exam takers. A student whose iPad fails mid-exam is told to notify the proctor immediately rather than troubleshoot, because the paper copy and the resume code are both in the room.

Note

Students cannot open the exam early

Even with the file downloaded 48 hours ahead, the exam will not open until the password is announced. There is no window in which a student holds an openable exam.

Section 11

After the exam#

Results arrive faster than most people expect. What to look at, and in what order.

Note

Performance is visible within minutes

Once students upload their answer files, the assessment overview in the portal gives you a first read on how the class did. You do not wait for a batch process or for OME to send you anything.

What to look at first

  1. Assessment overview Distribution and overall performance. This is your sanity check that the exam behaved as intended.
  2. Item analysis Find questions where the whole class failed or where the distractors did not discriminate. These are your candidates for scoring adjustment and for retirement before next year.
  3. Category performance This is what the category tags were for. A report can show category performance as a summary or broken out by exam taker, which is how you see whether a topic landed across the cohort.
Watch out

Scoring adjustments are a deliberate act

If an item was defective, scoring can be adjusted after the fact — accepting a second correct answer, or dropping the item. Do this in consultation with OME, not unilaterally, because it changes the record for every student in the cohort.

Note the annual loop: the item analysis you run this year is what tells you which questions to remove when you duplicate this exam next year. See Building an assessment.

Your account and permissions#

What OME sets on your account and why. Useful when something you expected to see is not there.

Your access is configured under Main Access Rights when OME creates the account. In the Legacy portal, most areas offer full or restricted, where restricted can be scoped to particular folders rather than the whole institution. The table below is the standard configuration for a new pre-clerkship course director.

Standard Main Access Rights for a new course director
AreaSettingReason
AdministrationRestrictedGives portal access without the ability to create other users.
QuestionsFull, or scoped to your foldersYou need to write and edit items. Folder scoping is used where a course should only touch its own bank.
AssessmentsFull, or scoped to your foldersYou need to build and edit assessments for your course.

Critical

Grading stays unchecked

Checking the Grading right turns the account into a grader-only account, which is not what a course director needs and will remove access you expect to have. If you have been given a grader account by mistake, the symptom is that you can see grading queues but not the question bank or assessment builder.

Watch out

Why Categories is restricted

Roughly nine years of item data is tagged against the shared category tree. Renaming or moving a node silently changes what past reports mean. If your course needs a category that does not exist, ask OME to add it rather than working around the restriction.

If the External ID does not match what UH SSO sends, sign-in fails with the account-not-found error described in Account and sign-in. That is the single most common access problem.

Students are told not to troubleshoot on their own, and the same applies to you. The proctor holds the resume code and the hard copies. Escalation after the fact is fine; improvisation during the exam is not.

Watch out

Check the title before you trust an article

Searching the support site returns Enterprise and Legacy versions of nearly every article, and they describe different screens. If the title does not start with Legacy Portal, or the article is not explicitly marked as covering both, it may not match what you see.

JABSOM runs the Legacy portal, not Enterprise. Vendor documentation exists in both flavors and the screens differ. Every vendor link on this page is the Legacy article.

Vendor tickets are filed by OME, not by course directors. If you hit something that looks like an ExamSoft defect, send it to Jesse rather than contacting ExamSoft directly, so the ticket carries the institution context.
